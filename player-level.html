How it works

Your Court Standing is derived from games played — the count every finished game already writes. Nothing new is stored, so every existing player wakes up already standing on the rung their history earned (a 100-game veteran logs in around Level 22, name already silver). Solo and multiplayer count alike; the tutorial's guided game doesn't. Star drops pay inside the same transaction as the game result, exactly once per rung.

The front door — the menu chip at each tier

The level roundel and the name's tint climb together every ten levels. The rank sits under the name in small caps. This is the piece everyone sees on the main page.

The ladder — all fifty rungs

A level a game to start, stretching to fifteen-game rungs in the forties. Landmarks: Level 10 ≈ 21 games · 20 ≈ 71 · 30 ≈ 151 · 40 ≈ 251 · 50 ≈ 401. Total star payout over the full climb: ★ 220.

The ranks, and why they sit in this order

Ordered as real life ranks them: commoner → gentry (Squire, Knight) → crown offices (Steward, Magistrate) → the peerage (Baron, Count, Earl) → the great offices that wield the crown's power and so outrank the nobles who hold land under it (a Chancellor runs the kingdom's law, a Viceroy rules in the monarch's name, a Regent rules the realm itself) → the crown. Herald takes Courtier's rung — the crown's own voice at court (Wyatt's call 7/30, since Knight and Duke/Duchess are heroes in the game: standing rule, no rank may share a name with a hero — now enforced by the test probe).

His & hers titles — the player chooses

Baron/Baroness, Count/Countess and King/Queen are a choice: the first time you earn one, the victory chip asks right there — “Choose your title: ” — and the choice carries to every later paired rank (a Baroness is later crowned Queen). It can be changed any time on the profile's Court Standing card. Until chosen, the rank shows both names. This is the one written piece of Court Standing state — a choice can't be derived.

The free table at Level 12

One of the six priced base skins — Crimson Velvet, The Queen's Table, Emerald Court, Midnight Atlas, The Cartographer's Desk, or Alchemist's Slate — chosen by a per-account deterministic shuffle, always one you haven't already bought. Never a rare table (those stay earned by deed) and never a hero table. In the store it reads “✦ A gift of the court.”

Where else the standing shows

Leaderboard: every name wears its tint on all boards (the row already carries the games count — zero schema change), with the rank as the hover tooltip. Not yet: other seats' tints at the live multiplayer table — the seat payload doesn't carry a games count today; noted as a follow-up if the tints earn their keep.
