The Ledger of Deeds

Forty-eight achievement ideas for FAVOR, grouped into eight chapters. Every one carries a number — point at the numbers you want and I'll build those. None of them pay anything. No stars, no tables, no crests: a deed is a name, a rank and the moment it fires. What each one does carry is how hard it is and what it costs us to build, because a deed that needs the engine to notice something new is a different job from one that ships tonight.

The vision why the game wants these

A tutorial in disguise

Most players will never borrow a skill from three rivals, never turn a board to Side B, never find out gold can go negative. A deed is a dare that teaches the mechanic without a single line of instruction.

A reason for the next game

Court Standing rewards playing at all. Hero levels reward playing one hero. Deeds reward playing differently — a hero you avoid, a strategy you'd never risk, a chapter of the Almanac that's still blank.

A trophy for the story

The funniest games are the disasters. Right now the game says nothing when you finish on minus fourteen. It should hand you a medal, and you should want to show somebody.

Where they live. The plumbing already exists and works: a deed is a pure check() against a snapshot of your seat, it grants in a single transaction on the player's own record, and it can never grant twice. The gallery on the title screen and the unlock ceremony at game over are built. What's missing is the content — and one new home: the collection deeds belong in the Almanac, printed on the chapter tabs, where a player already sees "18 of 30 discovered" and feels the gap.

How to read a deed

The five ranks, kept exactly as they are — but they now mean difficulty and nothing else. The rank a deed wears is the whole reward. Bronze is a first night; Legendary is a year.

What a deed pays nothing — and that's the design

No stars, no tables, no crests. A deed pays a name, a rank and the moment it fires. That's the whole reward, and it's the right one: the game already has three ladders that pay. Court Standing pays stars for playing at all. Hero levels pay tables and crests for devotion. The Mint sells the rest. Deeds become the fourth thing — the one that pays in reputation.

The practical gain is that every constraint disappears. Stars are real money; tie trophies to currency and each new idea turns into an economy negotiation, which is exactly how the funniest deeds die — nobody can justify paying a player thirty stars for finishing on minus fourteen. Unpriced, Rock Bottom costs nothing to grant, all forty-eight can ship, and the forty-ninth idea is free too.

ConsequenceWhat has to change
The rank needs to be real data Today a deed's tier is derived from its star value — stars >= 200 ? legendary : …. Set the new deeds to zero and every one of them comes out Bronze. So the rank becomes an explicit field on the deed. One line per entry, and the gallery keeps its five colours.
The grant gets simpler The transaction still writes the deed to the player's record — it just stops adding stars. Nothing about the once-per-account guarantee changes.
Two eras in one gallery The twenty-one deeds already in the game do pay — 570★ between them — and they stay exactly as they are; nobody's earned ledger gets devalued. A careful player will notice the old ones paid and the new ones don't. I'd leave it alone: that's history, not a bug.

Three decisions I need from you

1 · Does the Almanac follow the player, or the phone?

The book lives in the browser's storage on one device. Play on your phone and your laptop and you have two half-books; reinstall the app and the collection is gone. Every deed in chapter A inherits that.

My recommendation: sync the Almanac to the player record before we ship any collection deed — the same row, the same transaction pattern the achievements already use. It's the difference between a collection worth keeping and one that quietly evaporates. It also means chapter A can't ship in the first pass.

2 · Do disasters celebrate as loudly as victories?

Today an unlock is a gold card with a fanfare. "Rock Bottom" arriving in the same gold frame is funny once and wrong twice.

My recommendation: a second frame for ruin — cracked, ash-grey, its own sound. Cheap to build, and it's the thing people will screenshot.

3 · How many deeds ship in the first pass?

Nothing forces the answer any more — unpriced deeds cost the game nothing, so all forty-eight could land at once. The reason not to is pacing: a player with two hundred games behind them would unlock a dozen in a single evening and feel nothing for any of them.

My recommendation: wave 1 and the sharpest half of wave 2 — around twenty — then watch what actually fires before adding the rest. The back half is a data file away whenever you want it.
